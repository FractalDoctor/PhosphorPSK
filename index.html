△ PHOSPHORPSK

Real-Time PSK31 Decoder for iPhone.

PhosphorPSK decodes PSK31 (Phase Shift Keying) signals in real time using your iPhone's microphone. Hold your phone near a shortwave radio tuned to a PSK31 signal and watch the text appear on an authentic phosphor CRT display, character by character.

No internet connection required. No external hardware. No account. Just your iPhone and a radio.

FEATURES

  • Real-time BPSK decoding via microphone — PSK31, PSK63, and PSK125
  • Locks onto the strongest signal in the passband — isolate the one you want by tuning your radio
  • Canonical G3PLX Varicode, decoded exactly
  • Live FFT spectrum display with carrier cursor and scrolling waterfall
  • BPSK vector scope — watch the constellation snap into place as it locks
  • Carrier, SNR, level, and lock readouts
  • Session logging with full decoded text, timestamps, and signal parameters
  • Amber and green phosphor CRT themes with scanlines, vignette, and glow effects
  • Adjustable input gain, manual or automatic carrier tuning
  • No accounts, no tracking, no network access

DSP ENGINE

Complex numerically-controlled-oscillator downconvert to baseband, a two-stage matched filter (a raised-cosine filter then the G3PLX pskcore filter), amplitude-waveform symbol-timing recovery, and differential BPSK bit decision feeding a Varicode assembler. The receive chain is ported from fldigi, the reference PSK31 implementation. PhosphorPSK locks the strongest signal in the passband; you isolate the one you want by tuning your radio. All signal processing runs entirely on-device.

DECODER ACCURACY

PhosphorPSK has been tested against raised-cosine pulse-shaped synthetic signals and genuine off-air PSK31 recordings. Detailed test results, methodology, and decoded output are available in the decoder test report.

WHERE TO FIND PSK31

  • 20m band: around 14.070 MHz
  • 40m band: around 7.070 MHz
  • 80m band: around 3.580 MHz
  • 15m band: around 21.070 MHz
  • 30m band: around 10.142 MHz

Set the radio to USB and tune so the signal sits around 1 kHz of audio. A shortwave receiver, SDR, or WebSDR audio played through a speaker is all you need.
